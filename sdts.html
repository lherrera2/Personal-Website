SIX DEGREE OF FREEDOM DYNAMIC TEST SYSTEM

A real-time hardware-in-the-loop simulation environment used to validate spacecraft docking systems through controlled motion, sensing, and closed-loop dynamics.

SYSTEM OVERVIEW

The SDTS simulates spacecraft docking in a controlled ground environment.

A robotic motion platform drives a full-scale docking interface toward a fixed test article, while real-time simulation software computes system response.

This enables validation of docking systems before flight, reducing mission risk and ensuring performance under realistic conditions.

MOTION SYSTEM

The system is driven by an electric six-degree-of-freedom (e6DOF) Stewart platform developed by Mikrolar.

This robot replaces a legacy hydraulic system and provides precise motion in translation and rotation.

Docking hardware, such as the International Docking Adapter (IDA), is mounted to the robot and driven according to simulation commands.

The system supports payloads up to ~5000 kg with precise translational and rotational motion.

MEASUREMENT SYSTEMS

Laser Tracking (Leica)

Tracks position and orientation in six degrees of freedom at high frequency. Provides absolute position reference for the robot during testing and system setup.

Infrared Tracking (Optotrak)

Captures motion of infrared markers placed on docking hardware. Enables precise relative motion tracking between interfaces.

Force–Moment Sensors (ATI)

Measures forces and moments during contact between docking systems. Sensors are mounted on both motion and fixed load ring assemblies.

CONTROLS & SIMULATION

The SDTS operates as a closed-loop system using real-time simulation and control computers.

The Simhost computer calculates system dynamics and generates motion commands. The Digital Control System (DCS) executes actuator commands at high frequency.

During contact, measured forces from the load sensors are fed back into the simulation.

This creates a closed-loop system: motion → measurement → simulation → updated motion commands.

The result is a dynamic response that replicates real spacecraft docking behavior.

DOCKING TEST FLOW

1. Docking hardware is mounted to the robot and fixed structure

2. Measurement systems are calibrated (Leica, Optotrak, ATI)

3. Simulation is initialized through Simhost

4. Robot executes motion toward docking interface

5. Contact forces are measured during interaction

6. Feedback is processed in real time to adjust motion

7. Data is recorded for validation and certification

SUPPORT SYSTEMS

A large steel superstructure positions and supports the fixed docking interface with adjustable height and alignment.

Video systems provide real-time monitoring and high-definition recording of test operations.

Synchronized data acquisition ensures precise timing across all measurement and control systems.

ENGINEERING CONTRIBUTION

• Improved measurement reliability through Optotrak mounting redesign and Leica inverted design

• Developed multi-axis calibration tooling for force–moment sensors

• Supported real-time docking test execution and validation

• Designed mechanical hardware for system integration within SDTS

WHY IT MATTERS



The SDTS enables full-scale validation of spacecraft docking systems before flight.

By replicating real-world dynamics and contact behavior, it reduces mission risk and ensures reliable performance across multiple space programs.
