DEPLOYABLE SHIELD MECHANISM

Designed a lightweight deployable shielding system for spacecraft payloads, creating a scalable mechanism adaptable to 4, 6, and 8-sided geometries.

Impact

• Developed deployable mechanism adaptable to multiple geometries
• Demonstrated controlled expansion and retraction in prototype
• Validated feasibility of lightweight shielding system for spacecraft

Constraints

• Required adaptability for 4, 6, and 8-sided configurations
• Limited by 3D printing size constraints during prototyping
• Needed lightweight structure with reliable deployment

Approach

1. Defined geometric and deployment requirements
2. Developed modular linkage-based mechanism
3. Modeled system in CAD for multiple configurations
4. Built and tested segmented prototype

PROJECT CONTEXT

This project was sponsored by Ball Aerospace as part of a senior design program focused on deployable systems for spacecraft payload protection.

The objective was to develop a compact mechanism capable of expanding in orbit while remaining lightweight and adaptable to multiple configurations.

DESIGN CHALLENGE

The mechanism needed to support multiple payload geometries (4, 6, and 8 sides) while maintaining a compact stowed configuration.

Key requirements:

• Adaptability across configurations • Lightweight structure • Controlled deployment • Manufacturability within prototyping constraints

ENGINEERING DESIGN

Led CAD modeling using SolidWorks and Siemens NX to develop a modular deployable mechanism.

The design used linked structural elements to enable controlled expansion while maintaining alignment and stability across configurations.

PROTOTYPING CONSTRAINTS

Manufacturing limitations due to 3D printer size required redesigning large components into modular sections.

Parts were segmented and assembled post-print, maintaining functionality while enabling fabrication within constraints.

RESULT

Developed a functional prototype demonstrating controlled deployment and retraction of the shielding system.

The mechanism validated adaptability across multiple geometries and demonstrated feasibility for future development.
